# Reporting recipe on real documents (9 Nov 2026)

What was built: the owner's approved snapshot of one model report's links (`workroom_core/recipe.py`). A copy of the model found in the project inherits those links as ordinary links; each is then checked by the same rules as any link (the figure must still be where the model had it or have the same words around it, a source that moved is followed by its headings, a changed figure is held, anything that does not fit is dropped or asked). Agents may propose a recipe (`workroom_recipe_propose`) and read the status (`workroom_recipe_status`, in the read-only toolset); only the owner route (`recipe-approve`) installs it, bound to the digest the owner was shown, and it is refused if the model changed after the proposal. Ten tests in `tests/test_recipe.py`.

## The window refresh (built later the same day), and what it did on the real statements

A copy of last period's report against a workbook whose window of periods has slid (this year's file holds 2025 and 2024 where last year's held 2024 and 2023) can now be refreshed in one roll: `workroom_roll_forward` / the Desktop route take `window: true`. Inherited links remember the headings they were made under (`intent_labels`) and keep them until a release is prepared or rolled, so a roll starts from "last year's 2024 column" and finds "this year's 2025 column" by its headings, even though last year's 2023 column no longer exists. Figures whose new period the workbook lacks keep their old numbers and the release lists them; figures with no single period in their headings are left as written; a cell that holds only a year advances with its column. Two generated reports pass end to end (figures rewritten, the year headings moved with them, nothing left out of step afterwards; a workbook that lacks the newest year rolls what it can and says so).

On the real DEECA statements (2023-24 statements and workbook, copied and refreshed against the 2024-25 workbook) the first try rolled 97 of about 3,200 inherited links. The cause was in the workbook reader: the statements print their years as numbers (2024 | 2023, or a year alone on a row that opens a block of rows), and a number was never read as a heading, so most cells carried no period at all. The reader now takes a year printed as a number as a column heading (even with a unit row such as $'000 between) or as the opener of a block of rows. Headings are also compared without footnote marks ("Agriculture(i)" one year, "Agriculture" the next). The same run now rolls 1,286 figures; 1,271 keep their old figure because the new workbook has no value for them (output groups the department dropped between the years), and the rest are left as written (figures under no single period, figures linked by value, figures that are not year figures). A rolled figure that grows or turns negative now follows the document's own style (3,893 and (59,816), not 3893 and -59816); that gap was found in this run.

Scored against the department's published 2024-25 report by table, row and column position: 1,254 figures changed, 231 equal the published figure, 914 have no counterpart at the same position, and 109 differ from the published figure at the same position. All 109 sit in four notes (4.1.2, 4.1.3, 4.2.1, 4.2.2) whose columns the department regrouped between the years (the Building group disappears, Effective Water Management appears), so the position test cannot say whether they are right; NOT VERIFIED. Outside those four notes no changed figure disagrees with the published report. A second, stricter scoring keys each figure by section, row heading and the column-group heading (read with python-docx, independent of the product's reader, `oracle_groups.py`), which lines the regrouped notes up by the group's name: 1,133 figures changed; 291 equal the published figure, 827 have no counterpart, 15 differ. The 15 are in note 7.5.1 (repeated row labels and columns with identical headings, so the pairing is ambiguous for the scorer and for the product alike), the total columns of note 4.1.3, and one in 5.1.1; traced against the 2024-25 workbook: in three of them (the 7.5.1 lease and advances rows and their total) the published figure appears nowhere in the workbook, so the published report and the department's own workbook disagree and the refreshed copy follows the workbook; in the other twelve both figures exist in the workbook, which points to the scorer pairing figures from different tables of one section (the key has no table identity), but that was not proven table by table (NOT VERIFIED). They are listed in the result file.

A roll now also checks every column that becomes a period the report already showed: when the workbook gives a different figure for that period than the report did, the release lists it as restated or a row that moved (`restated` in the roll result and the release record). On DEECA this listed 16 figures; for four of the differing figures above (three in 7.5.1, one in 4.1.3) the copy's prior-year column agrees with last year's published current-year figure and the published 2024-25 report's does not. The check cannot catch a report whose rows are shifted against the workbook in both of its columns, and in the other differing figures it says nothing. Of the 1,103 figures that differ between the two published years under this key, 291 were updated and 801 stayed as last year. Coverage by position: of the 1,269 figures that differ between the two published years at comparable positions, 231 were updated and 929 stayed as last year; the rest of the changed figures cannot be scored by position. A scored claim for the whole report is therefore not made.

Reader change checked against the three adjudicated sets (the figures the human and independent readers judged): statements, the accepted-and-right count stays 112 and one wrong automatic link becomes a question; annual report, accepted-and-right 108 and 8 correct links asked, as before; Health, 120 of 120 accepted links right, unchanged. The full-run counts moved slightly (statements accepted 3,053 to 3,026, asked 1,003 to 1,019; annual report accepted 343 to 345; Health 4,307 to 4,310). These sets were already development data, so this is a regression check, not a new blind result.

What would make it work better, not built: matching an old workbook to a new one by output-group name when the department regroups, so renamed groups ("Agriculture(i)" to "Agriculture") and dropped groups are mapped once and reused.
